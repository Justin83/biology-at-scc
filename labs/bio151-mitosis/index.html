How to count

  1. Find a well-preserved blastula preparation under low power.
  2. Increase magnification until you can identify chromosomes clearly.
  3. Choose Sample A and count 25 identifiable cells systematically.
  4. Record the number of cells in each stage.
  5. Move to a different, nonoverlapping area for Sample B and count another 25 cells.
  6. Check that both totals equal 25, then save.

Count every identifiable cell in the area, not only the dividing ones. If an area has fewer than 25 identifiable cells, continue into an adjacent area that doesn't overlap. Count each cell only once. If you can't classify a cell reliably, skip it and keep going until you reach 25.

Stage clues
Interphase
Nucleus visible; chromatin fairly diffuse; no clearly condensed chromosomes.
Prophase
Chromosomes condense and become visible as darker structures.
Metaphase
Condensed chromosomes lined up near the cell's equator.
Anaphase
Sister chromatids separate into two groups moving toward opposite poles.
Telophase
Chromosomes reach opposite poles and new nuclei begin forming. Count cells visibly undergoing cytokinesis as telophase.
