Allen Branch

A small stream mapped by the U.S. Geological Survey a short distance from Somerset Community College's main campus at 808 Monticello Street, Somerset, Kentucky. It is the portal's first research site and the place where SCC students will build a record of a local freshwater system from the ground up.

What this profile does not claim

  • No water-quality measurement, organism record or photograph from Allen Branch exists yet.
  • Allen Branch is not a documented cyanobacterial bloom site.
  • The downstream connection is the USGS-mapped path; it has not been walked or confirmed at different flows, and karst may divert water underground.
  • No upstream drainage area, land-cover percentage, agricultural influence or pollution source has been determined. Nearby land use is not evidence of what reaches the stream.
  • No campus access coordinates are published until faculty record them on site.

Watershed investigation checklist

The research tasks that turn this profile from mapped facts into field-confirmed knowledge. Status changes only when documentation is added.

  1. Confirm campus access coordinates – Not started

    Record a GPS position (with accuracy) for the safe access point used by classes, plus the route and any permissions needed.

  2. Verify mapped stream identity ◆ Mapped

    NHD names the flowline 'Allen Branch' (GNIS 00485848). Still to do: confirm that the channel reached from campus is this mapped line, and check county and local sources for other names.

    Documentation: NHD flowline layer (external site, opens in a new tab)

  3. Delineate upstream drainage – Not started

    Delineate the basin above defined sampling points with USGS StreamStats; record date, tool version and the pour-point coordinates.

    Documentation: USGS StreamStats (external site, opens in a new tab)

  4. Identify downstream confluences ◆ Mapped

    NHD maps Allen Branch joining Sinking Creek, which flows to Pitman Creek. Field verification of the junction and of flow continuity at different seasons is pending.

    Documentation: Mapped path on the site map

  5. Determine ultimate receiving waters ◆ Mapped

    NHD routes the mapped path into Lake Cumberland on the Cumberland River (Ohio River basin). Surface mapping only.

  6. Assess agricultural land cover – Not started

    After delineation, summarize NLCD and Cropland Data Layer classes inside the basin in QGIS. Report areas with their data year and resolution. Land cover alone will not be used to infer pollution.

    Documentation: Cropland Data Layer (external site, opens in a new tab)

  7. Identify possible stormwater inputs – Not started

    Inventory pipes, ditches and culverts entering the channel during a dry-weather walk; photograph and GPS each one. Possible inputs are listed as possible until flow is observed.

  8. Examine karst connectivity ▸ In progress

    KGS maps place the corridor on Mississippian limestones with 'intense' to 'prone' karst potential, and NHD maps springs, sinks and underground conduits nearby — though none on the Allen Branch line itself. Next: check KGS sinkhole and spring layers near the channel and look for losing or gaining reaches in the field.

    Documentation: KGS karst potential service (external site, opens in a new tab)

  9. Establish sampling stations – Not started

    Choose safe, repeatable stations (for example upstream, near campus, and above the Sinking Creek junction), each with a code, GPS position and photo.

  10. Conduct field verification – Not started

    First faculty-led reconnaissance: confirm channel location, flow state, canopy, substrate and access, and record the visit as a field observation.

Access and safety

Field safety rules →
Access not yet documented The campus access route, permission requirements and a safe entry point have not been recorded. Until faculty publish them here, visit Allen Branch only with a faculty member.
Before and during every visit
  • Do not enter the water during or after heavy rain, or when flow is high or turbid.
  • Wear gloves for water, sediment and biofilm; wash hands afterward.
  • Do not touch or collect surface scums or thick mats; photograph them and tell faculty.
  • Watch footing on wet rock and banks; stay clear of culverts and road crossings.
  • Report dead fish or animals rather than sampling them.

Research questions for this site

All questions →

  • Light and habitat ○ Proposed

    Do benthic cyanobacteria make up a larger share of the biofilm in sunlit stream reaches than in shaded reaches of the same stream?

    Background: Ch. 3 Freshwater ecology · Methods: canopy, biofilm · Start reading: Wood et al. (2020); Quiblier et al. (2013); Loftin et al. (2016)

  • Nutrients and communities ○ Proposed

    Are nitrate and orthophosphate concentrations in Allen Branch related to the composition of its microbial biofilm?

    Background: Ch. 4 Nutrients & watersheds · Methods: water chemistry · Start reading: Dodds & Smith (2016) (external site, opens in a new tab); Elser et al. (2007); Crain & Martin (2009)

  • Seasonality ○ Proposed

    How do benthic biofilms in Allen Branch change from spring to fall, and do cyanobacteria become more common as flow declines?

    Background: Ch. 3 Freshwater ecology · Methods: biofilm, flow · Start reading: Wood et al. (2020); Sommer et al. (2012) (external site, opens in a new tab)

  • Land use and stream chemistry ○ Proposed

    Once its drainage area is delineated, how does land cover in the Allen Branch basin compare with nearby basins, and does stream chemistry differ accordingly?

    Background: Ch. 4 Nutrients & watersheds · Methods: gis · Start reading: Carpenter et al. (1998); Crain & Martin (2009); Currens (2002)
