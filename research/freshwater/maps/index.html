Watersheds & Maps

Explore Allen Branch and the land around it using public map services from the U.S. Geological Survey, the Kentucky Geological Survey and the Commonwealth of Kentucky. Layers are requested live from each provider, so nothing is copied or redrawn here — and every layer states who made it, when, at what resolution, and what it cannot show.

Four kinds of geographic information

Evidence standards →

For Allen Branch, this portal keeps four kinds of information separate, and never lets one pass for another.

◆ Mapped Verified geographic facts

Supported by authoritative geographic data. Currently: the stream's name and NHD classification (intermittent), its mapped length (4.02 km), its mapped flow direction, the mapped path to Sinking Creek, Pitman Creek and Lake Cumberland, the hydrologic units it lies in, and the bedrock and karst classes along it.

○ Unverified Preliminary interpretations

Inferred from imagery, terrain or descriptions. Currently: "shaded stream corridor." Imagery and hillshade are useful for planning, but a channel seen under canopy or a depression on a hillshade is an interpretation until visited.

✓ Field confirmed Field observations

Recorded directly at a known location, with GPS position and accuracy. Currently: none. The first faculty-led visit will begin this category.

Hypothesis Research hypotheses

Proposed relationships that need testing — for example, that flow in Allen Branch is seasonal, or that some of its water arrives through karst. Listed on the Research Projects page, never shown as map facts.

Where Allen Branch sits in the drainage network

The USGS Watershed Boundary Dataset divides the country into nested hydrologic units, each identified by a code that gains two digits at each level. The mapped Allen Branch line lies entirely within one 12-digit subwatershed:

  • Region 05 — Ohio
  • HUC 8: 05130103 — Upper Cumberland–Lake Cumberland
  • HUC 10: 0513010302 — Pitman Creek–Cumberland River
  • HUC 12: 051301030204 — Lower Pitman Creek (128.26 km² for the whole unit)

In the National Hydrography Dataset, Allen Branch (GNIS 00485848) flows into Sinking Creek, which the dataset routes into Pitman Creek and then through Lake Cumberland on the Cumberland River. All of this is mapped information from USGS services checked on 2026-11-05; none of it has been verified in the field. The 128 km² area belongs to the whole Lower Pitman Creek unit — it is not the drainage area of Allen Branch, which has not been delineated.

Karst: when groundwater ignores the map

See Figure 4.1 →

Much of south-central Kentucky is underlain by limestone, which slowly dissolves to form sinkholes, springs, caves and sinking streams. In such karst terrain, water that falls in one surface watershed can travel underground through conduits and emerge in another. The Kentucky Geological Survey's statewide map rates the bedrock along the mapped Allen Branch corridor as having intense to prone karst potential, and its 1:24,000 geologic maps show Upper Mississippian limestones there.

Two consequences follow for anyone using this map. First, surface watershed boundaries and stream lines describe where water would go over the land surface, not necessarily where it does go. Second, nothing about groundwater paths at Allen Branch can be concluded from these layers; only field evidence — observing where flow is gained or lost, chemistry, and, in karst, dye tracing by qualified investigators — can establish them. Turn on Karst potential, Mapped sinkholes and Springs in the layer panel to see the regional context.

Not available yet

  • Culverts and road crossings. No public statewide culvert inventory was found. Roads appear on the USGS Topo base map; culverts on Allen Branch need a field inventory.
  • Upstream drainage area of Allen Branch. Not yet delineated. To be produced with USGS StreamStats (or lidar-based delineation in QGIS) and stored with its method and date.
  • Stormwater outfalls (campus and city). Not available as a public layer found so far; requires municipal records or field inventory.
  • Complete discharge-permit (KPDES) facilities. The wastewater layer above is an infrastructure inventory, not the full permit list. EPA ECHO facility search is the next source to integrate.
  • Allen Branch sampling stations. None established yet. Stations will be added after field reconnaissance with GPS coordinates and stated accuracy.

Rules this portal follows for maps

  • No watershed area, land-cover percentage or distance is calculated without real spatial data and a stated method.
  • No flow path, tributary connection or groundwater route is drawn unless an authoritative dataset shows it — and then it is labeled Mapped, not confirmed.
  • Nearby land use, facilities or farms are never treated as evidence of what reaches a stream.
  • Large datasets are used through their providers' live services rather than copied; the only local geometries are small, labeled extracts in data/geo/.

To go further, use USGS StreamStats for drainage delineation, the Kentucky Watershed Explorer, the National Map Viewer and QGIS.
